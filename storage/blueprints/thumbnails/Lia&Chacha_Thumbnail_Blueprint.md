🔒 STYLE LOCK — NON-NEGOTIABLE

Bright, cheerful, and ultra-colorful 3D animation-style thumbnails for a children's educational channel. The visual language is playful, warm, and instantly recognizable — conveying fun learning experiences, family togetherness, and musical enjoyment for toddlers and preschoolers. Thumbnails feature beloved 3D-animated characters (Lia, ChaCha, Andy, Mom Lily, Dad Jake) in vibrant, bubblegum-colored scenes with simple, readable text that promises engaging songs and learning moments. The overall vibe is wholesome, energetic, and child-friendly — not dark, not cluttered, never scary or overly complex.

❌ Not allowed:

- Dark, moody, or muted color palettes (keep it bright and saturated)
- Realistic or photographic imagery (everything must be 3D animation style)
- Cluttered frames with more than 4–5 distinct visual elements
- Text that exceeds 4–5 words total on the thumbnail
- Scary, aggressive, or intense expressions on characters
- Complex backgrounds that distract from the characters
- Any imagery that could frighten or confuse young children

🧍‍♂️ FRAMING & POSE

Primary subject: One or more of the core Lia & ChaCha family characters — typically:

- **Lia** — the main female character, a 2-year-old girl with signature red outfit, two bouncy ponytails with red bows, and a plush fox toy.
- **ChaCha** — Lia's twin brother, a 2-year-old boy with signature blue outfit, three short hair tufts on top, and a plush lion toy.
- **Andy** — the older brother, 5 years old, with a rocket emblem badge on his chest.
- **Lily** — the mom, with reddish‑brown hair in a side braid, often holding a camera or smartphone.
- **Jake** — the dad, wearing a kitchen apron, always with a warm smile.
- Occasional guest characters or animal friends.

Framing: Medium to close-up shots. Characters occupy 50–70% of the frame, filling the center or positioned slightly off-center. Multiple characters may appear together, interacting with each other or with objects. When showing twins, they are often placed side by side or facing each other.

Angle: Straight-on, eye-level perspective — warm and inviting, making direct visual contact with the young viewer. Characters face the camera or look toward the action.

Energy: High-energy, joyful, and enthusiastic. Characters display strong, exaggerated expressions — wide smiles, surprised eyes, excited open mouths. This emotional clarity is critical for attracting young viewers' attention.

Cropping: Generous — characters are shown from the chest/waist up or full-body with plenty of breathing room. Never cropped tightly against the edges.

🎨 BACKGROUND & LIGHTING

Environment: Bright, cheerful, and simple settings — typically:

- Solid or softly gradient backgrounds in bright colors (sky blue, sunny yellow, soft pink, mint green)
- Simple contextual scenes — a bedroom, a playground, a garden, a bathroom, a kitchen (Jake's restaurant), a farm
- Minimal props that relate to the video's theme (toys, bath items, food, musical instruments, the plush fox/lion, cooking utensils)

Lighting: Bright, even, shadowless illumination — consistent with 3D animation. No dramatic shadows, no harsh highlights. Everything feels sunny and warm.

Depth: Moderate — characters are clearly separated from the background, with subtle depth cues (shadows on the floor, slight perspective). The world feels 3D but simple and approachable.

Visual noise: Very low — backgrounds are clean and uncluttered, allowing the characters and any text to stand out clearly.

🚨 VISUAL ATTENTION ELEMENT

Usage: Minimal and organic. The channel relies on the characters' expressive faces, bright colors, and simple text to attract attention.

If used:

- A character pointing toward something
- A character holding up an object related to the video's theme (e.g., Lia holding her fox, ChaCha holding his lion, Andy showing a rocket)
- A simple sparkle or glow effect around a key element
- No arrows, no circles, no emojis, no neon glow effects

Rule: The "attention" comes from the characters' emotions and the bright, inviting visuals — not from aggressive graphic elements. Everything should feel natural and child-friendly.

📝 TEXT STYLE

Structure:

- Maximum 1 line of text (sometimes 2 very short lines)
- Positioned prominently — often centered at the bottom or along the lower third
- Text occupies approximately 10–15% of the thumbnail area

Typography:

- Bold, rounded, sans-serif font (e.g., Comic Sans MS, KG Primary Penmanship, or similar child-friendly rounded font)
- Primary text: White or bright yellow, with a strong dark outline or drop shadow for contrast against bright backgrounds
- Occasionally text appears in colored text boxes (blue, red, yellow) for additional contrast

Hierarchy:

- Line 1: The song/activity title (largest, boldest) — 2–4 words max
- Line 2 (if used): Supporting context (smaller) — 1–3 words max

Background for text: Often a solid colored bar (blue, red, yellow) or semi-transparent dark overlay at the bottom of the image to ensure text readability against bright backgrounds.

🎯 COMPOSITION RULES

Rule of thirds: Characters are centered or positioned slightly off-center, filling the middle and lower thirds. Text occupies the bottom third. The background provides simple context in the upper portion.

Focal hierarchy:

- Primary: The character(s)' faces (emotional connection with young viewers)
- Secondary: Text (the title/hook for parents)
- Tertiary: Supporting visual elements (props, objects, simple background details)

Negative space: Generous use of empty space around the characters and text — nothing feels cramped or overwhelming for young eyes.

Distraction control: Maximum 3–4 distinct visual elements total (character(s), text, 1–2 simple props/background elements).

Mobile-first: Text must be large enough to read on a smartphone screen. Characters' faces must be clearly visible and emotionally expressive even at small sizes.
