🔒 STYLE LOCK — NON-NEGOTIABLE

Epic, cinematic, space-focused thumbnails for a channel covering NASA missions, lunar exploration, and the new space race. The visual language is grand, awe-inspiring, and slightly mysterious — conveying the scale and significance of humanity's expansion beyond Earth. Thumbnails feature striking space imagery (planets, rockets, lunar surfaces, astronauts) combined with bold, urgent text that promises breaking developments and historic revelations. The overall vibe is dramatic, authoritative, and forward-looking — not cartoonish, not overly technical/dry, and never cluttered with excessive elements.

❌ Not allowed:

Cartoonish or overly simplified illustrations (use cinematic/realistic space imagery)

Dark, muddy, or low-contrast compositions (space imagery should pop with deep blacks and bright highlights)

Cluttered frames with more than 3–4 distinct visual elements

Text that exceeds 5–6 words total on the thumbnail

Overly technical jargon in the text (keep it accessible and dramatic)

Amateurish or low-quality space imagery (must look polished and cinematic)

🧍‍♂️ FRAMING & POSE

Primary subject: Dominant space imagery — typically one of the following:

A planet or celestial body (Earth, Moon, Mars) filling a significant portion of the frame

A rocket or spacecraft in dramatic angle (launch, ascending, or orbiting)

An astronaut in silhouette or facing away from camera toward a cosmic scene

A lunar surface or space station with Earth in the background

Framing: Wide, cinematic compositions that emphasize scale. The primary subject occupies 40–60% of the frame, with the remaining space used for text and atmospheric depth.

Angle: Dramatic angles — low-angle shots of rockets launching, wide shots of planets with curvature visible, or first-person perspective looking out into space. Not flat or straight-on.

Energy: High-stakes, urgent, and awe-inspiring. The imagery conveys "something historic is happening right now."

Cropping: Subjects are framed with breathing room for text placement, typically on one side or at the bottom of the frame.

🎨 BACKGROUND & LIGHTING

Environment: Deep space with stars, planetary surfaces, atmospheric glow, or Earth's curvature. The background often features:

Dark space with scattered stars and galaxies

Earth's atmosphere with blue glow and cloud patterns

Lunar or planetary surfaces with craters and horizon lines

Launch pads with dramatic sky backgrounds (sunset/sunrise lighting)

Lighting: High-contrast cinematic lighting — deep blacks in space, bright highlights on planets and spacecraft, dramatic backlighting or rim lighting on subjects. Often uses a "golden hour" or sunrise/sunset color palette for Earth-based launch imagery.

Depth: Strong sense of depth — layered compositions with foreground (astronaut/rocket), midground (planet/surface), and background (deep space/stars).

Visual noise: Low to moderate — space imagery is inherently dramatic but should not feel chaotic. Stars, planets, and spacecraft are the primary visual elements.

🧭 IDENTIFICADORES / SÍMBOLOS DO CANAL

Recurring visual symbols:

NASA logo or official agency insignia (when relevant to the video topic)

American flag on spacecraft or lunar surfaces (context-dependent)

Rocket plumes / exhaust trails — dramatic fire and smoke

Orbital trajectories — curved lines showing flight paths

Lunar modules or space stations (ISS, Gateway, etc.)

Earth as a contextual element (showing scale or perspective)

Placement: Symbols are integrated naturally into the space imagery — not overlaid as stickers or afterthoughts. They appear as part of the scene (e.g., flag on a lunar module, logo on a rocket).

🚨 VISUAL ATTENTION ELEMENT

Usage: Minimal and intentional. The space imagery itself provides the visual impact.

If used:

A subtle glow or highlight around a key element (e.g., a specific region of the Moon, a rocket's trajectory)

A red circle or arrow only for pointing out a specific detail in the image (rare)

Text serves as the primary attention-grabbing element, not graphic overlays

Rule: The drama comes from the space imagery itself — not from aggressive clickbait graphics. Use attention elements sparingly and only when necessary to guide the viewer to a specific detail.

📝 TEXT STYLE

Structure:

Maximum 2 lines of text

Positioned prominently — often at the bottom center, bottom left, or along the horizon line of the image

Text occupies approximately 15–20% of the thumbnail area

Typography:

Bold, clean sans-serif font (e.g., Impact, Montserrat Bold, or similar heavy font)

Primary text: White or bright yellow/orange, with a strong dark outline or drop shadow for contrast against space backgrounds

Secondary text (if used): Smaller, in a contrasting color (e.g., red for urgency, blue for authority)

Hierarchy:

Line 1: Primary hook (largest, boldest) — 3–5 words max

Line 2: Supporting context (smaller) — 2–4 words max

Background for text: Often a semi-transparent dark bar or gradient fade behind the text to ensure readability against bright or complex space backgrounds. Sometimes text is placed directly on a dark area of the image (deep space) with no background bar.

🧠 TEXT PSYCHOLOGY

Tone: Urgent, historic, and curiosity-driven. The text promises exclusive insights, breaking developments, and game-changing revelations about space exploration.

🎯 COMPOSITION RULES

Rule of thirds: Primary subject (planet, rocket, astronaut) placed on one of the vertical or horizontal thirds. Text occupies the negative space — often at the bottom or on the opposite side.

Focal hierarchy:

Primary: The dramatic space imagery (planet, rocket, lunar surface) — the "wow" factor

Secondary: Text (the hook/promise)

Tertiary: Supporting visual details (stars, Earth in background, trajectory lines)

Negative space: Deep space provides natural negative space. Text and subjects are placed to avoid overlapping in ways that reduce readability.

Distraction control: Maximum 3–4 distinct visual elements total (primary subject, secondary space element, text, optional attention graphic).

Mobile-first: Text must be large enough to read on a smartphone screen. The primary subject must be clearly identifiable even at small sizes.

Aspect ratio:
16:9

Resolution:
1280 × 720 minimum

Image style: Cinematic, realistic, or high-quality CGI/rendered space imagery — not 2D illustration or cartoon style. The aesthetic should match high-end space documentaries or NASA's official imagery.

Technical restrictions: No cartoonish illustrations. No low-quality stock photos. Imagery should feel current, polished, and cinematic. Color grading should be dramatic but natural — deep space blacks, rich planetary colors, warm atmospheric glows.
