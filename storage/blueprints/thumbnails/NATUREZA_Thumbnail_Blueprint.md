🧍‍♂️ FRAMING & POSE

Primary subject: Close-up, tactile imagery of plants, crops, and natural elements — typically one of the following:

A single featured plant or crop — shown in detail (leaves, roots, flowers, fruit), often held in human hands

Hands working with soil, seeds, or plants — conveying practical knowledge and connection to the earth

A harvested food item — shown in its natural state, often contrasted with store-bought alternatives

A historical or archaeological element — ancient tools, ruins, or traditional growing methods

The natural environment — forests, fields, gardens, or wild landscapes where the featured plant grows

Framing: Close-up to medium shots. The primary subject occupies 50–70% of the frame, filling the center or positioned slightly off-center. Hands and plants are shown in intimate detail, inviting the viewer to examine the subject closely.

Angle: Straight-on or slightly downward (as if examining the plant yourself). Eye-level with the subject creates a sense of direct connection and hands-on learning.

Energy: Calm, curious, and instructive — the imagery conveys "this knowledge has been hidden from you" and "you can do this yourself." Not frantic or aggressive.

Cropping: Generous — subjects are shown with enough context to understand what they are, but close enough to see details. Never cropped so tightly that the subject becomes unrecognizable.

🎨 BACKGROUND & LIGHTING

Environment: Natural, outdoor settings — typically:

Forest floors, garden beds, or wild landscapes — where the featured plant grows naturally

Wooden tables, rustic surfaces — for harvested plants and food items

Soil, compost, or natural ground — grounding the imagery in the earth

Simple, blurred natural backgrounds — letting the subject stand out

Lighting: Natural, diffused daylight — soft shadows, warm tones. Often golden hour (early morning or late afternoon) light that feels warm and inviting. No harsh studio lighting, no artificial feel.

Depth: Moderate — foreground (subject/plant), midground (hands or context), background (natural environment). Creates a sense of being right there in the field or garden.

Visual noise: Low to moderate — backgrounds are natural and may contain some texture (leaves, soil, sky), but nothing that distracts from the primary subject.

🚨 VISUAL ATTENTION ELEMENT

Usage: Minimal and natural. The channel relies on the inherent curiosity of the featured plant and the promise of hidden knowledge, not aggressive graphics.

If used:

A subtle glow or highlight around the featured plant

A gentle arrow or circle pointing to a specific detail (rare)

No emojis, no neon effects, no aggressive arrows

Rule: The "attention" comes from the subject itself — an unusual plant, a forgotten crop, a surprising food — not from visual gimmicks. Keep it authentic and educational.

📝 TEXT STYLE

Structure:

Maximum 2 lines of text

Positioned prominently — often centered at the bottom, lower third, or along the side

Text occupies approximately 15–20% of the thumbnail area

Typography:

Bold, clean, sans-serif font (e.g., Montserrat, Roboto, or similar modern font) — or a slightly rustic/earthy serif for warmth

Primary text: White or light-colored, with a strong dark outline or drop shadow for contrast against natural backgrounds

Secondary text (if used): Smaller, in a contrasting earthy color (golden, green, or white)

Hierarchy:

Line 1: Primary hook (largest, boldest) — 3–5 words max

Line 2: Supporting context (smaller) — 2–4 words max

Background for text: Often a semi-transparent dark overlay or gradient fade at the bottom of the image to ensure text readability against varied natural backgrounds. Sometimes text is placed directly on a darker area of the image.

🎯 COMPOSITION RULES

Rule of thirds: Primary subject (plant, hands, crop) placed centrally or slightly off-center. Text occupies the bottom third. The natural background provides context in the upper portion.

Focal hierarchy:

Primary: The featured plant or crop (the "hook")

Secondary: Text (the promise/hook)

Tertiary: Supporting visual details (hands, soil, tools, natural environment)

Negative space: Moderate — enough room around the subject and text to avoid clutter, but the subject fills a significant portion of the frame.

Distraction control: Maximum 3–4 distinct visual elements total (primary subject, text, 1–2 supporting elements like hands or tools).

Mobile-first: Text must be large enough to read on a smartphone screen. The primary subject (the plant) must be clearly identifiable even at small sizes.
