Warm, engaging, and educational thumbnails for a channel dedicated to decoding cat behavior and feline psychology. The visual language is approachable, curiosity-driven, and emotionally resonant — conveying the mystery and charm of cats while promising clear, science-backed insights into their behavior. Thumbnails feature expressive cats in relatable everyday situations (staring, sleeping, following, ignoring), often combined with bold, curiosity-driven text that promises to reveal the "hidden meaning" behind common feline behaviors. The overall vibe is friendly, trustworthy, and slightly playful — not cold or clinical, not overly dramatic, and never cluttered or chaotic.

❌ Not allowed:

Cold, clinical, or overly scientific aesthetics (keep it warm and accessible)

Dark, gloomy, or menacing imagery (cats should look cute or intriguing, not scary)

Cluttered frames with more than 3–4 distinct visual elements

Text that exceeds 6–8 words total on the thumbnail

Aggressive "clickbait" graphics (no neon arrows, no shock expressions)

Generic stock photos of cats that don't feel personal or relatable

Overly polished or studio-style photography — keep it natural and authentic

🧍‍♂️ FRAMING & POSE

Primary subject: A cat (or multiple cats) displaying a specific behavior or expression — typically one of the following:

A cat staring directly at the camera — creating a sense of connection and mystery

A cat in a relatable everyday pose — sleeping, following, ignoring, kneading, zooming

A close-up of a cat's face — highlighting eyes, ears, or whiskers that communicate emotion

A cat interacting with its environment — pushing things off surfaces, staring at nothing, etc.

A cat with a human element — hands petting, a lap, or the cat observing the owner

Framing: Close-up to medium shots. The cat occupies 50–70% of the frame, filling the center or positioned slightly off-center. The cat's face and eyes are the primary focal point, creating emotional connection with the viewer.

Angle: Eye-level with the cat — creating a sense of equality and connection. Sometimes slightly below (looking up at the cat) to emphasize curiosity, or slightly above to show the cat in its environment.

Energy: Calm, curious, and slightly mysterious — the imagery conveys "your cat is trying to tell you something" and "there's more to this behavior than meets the eye."

Cropping: Generous — cats are shown with enough context to understand the situation, but close enough to see facial expressions and body language cues. Never cropped so tightly that the cat's expression becomes unclear.

🎨 BACKGROUND & LIGHTING

Environment: Warm, familiar, everyday settings — typically:

Inside a home — living rooms, bedrooms, kitchens, hallways

Cozy, natural lighting — soft window light, warm lamps, or golden-hour sunlight

Simple, uncluttered backgrounds — letting the cat stand out

Occasional outdoor settings — gardens, patios, or windowsills

Lighting: Warm, soft, natural lighting — often from a window or lamp. Creates a cozy, intimate atmosphere. No harsh studio lighting, no artificial feel.

Depth: Moderate — foreground (cat), midground (contextual elements like furniture or floor), background (room or outdoor setting). Creates a sense of being right there with the cat.

Visual noise: Low — backgrounds are clean and uncluttered, allowing the cat and any text to stand out clearly.

🧭 IDENTIFICADORES / SÍMBOLOS DO CANAL

Recurring visual symbols:

The cat itself — the channel's recurring "star" (likely a specific cat or cats that viewers recognize)

Cat body language cues — tail position, ear position, pupil dilation, whisker movement

Everyday cat behaviors — sleeping, staring, following, ignoring, kneading, zoomies

Human elements — hands, laps, furniture — showing the cat-human relationship

Simple icons — paw prints, hearts, thought bubbles (used sparingly)

Placement: The cat is the primary visual focus. Additional elements are integrated naturally into the scene — not overlaid as stickers.

Color symbolism:

Warm tones — comfort, trust, home

Soft neutrals — authenticity, simplicity

Cat's natural colors — orange, black, white, grey, calico

🚨 VISUAL ATTENTION ELEMENT

Usage: Minimal and purposeful. The channel relies on the cat's expressive face and the curiosity-driven text, not aggressive graphics.

If used:

A subtle highlight or glow around the cat's eyes or face

A gentle arrow or circle pointing to a specific behavioral detail (rare)

No emojis, no neon effects, no aggressive arrows

Rule: The "attention" comes from the cat's expression and the promise of hidden meaning — not from visual gimmicks. Keep it authentic and educational.

📝 TEXT STYLE

Structure:

Maximum 2 lines of text

Positioned prominently — often centered at the bottom, lower third, or along the side

Text occupies approximately 15–20% of the thumbnail area

Typography:

Bold, clean, sans-serif font (e.g., Montserrat, Roboto, or similar modern font) — friendly and readable

Primary text: White or light-colored, with a strong dark outline or drop shadow for contrast

Secondary text (if used): Smaller, in a contrasting warm color (orange, yellow, or white)

Hierarchy:

Line 1: Primary hook (largest, boldest) — 3–5 words max

Line 2: Supporting context (smaller) — 2–4 words max

Background for text: Often a semi-transparent dark overlay or gradient fade at the bottom of the image to ensure text readability against varied backgrounds.

🎯 COMPOSITION RULES

Rule of thirds: Primary subject (cat) placed centrally or slightly off-center. Text occupies the bottom third. The background provides context in the upper portion.

Focal hierarchy:

Primary: The cat's face and expression (emotional connection)

Secondary: Text (the promise/hook)

Tertiary: Supporting visual details (environment, body language cues)

Negative space: Moderate — enough room around the cat and text to avoid clutter, but the cat fills a significant portion of the frame.

Distraction control: Maximum 3–4 distinct visual elements total (cat, text, 1–2 supporting elements like background context).

Mobile-first: Text must be large enough to read on a smartphone screen. The cat's face must be clearly visible and emotionally expressive even at small sizes.
