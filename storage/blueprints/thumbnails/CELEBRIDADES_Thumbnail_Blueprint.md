🔒 STYLE LOCK — NON-NEGOTIABLE

Dramatic, intrigue-driven, and emotionally charged thumbnails for a channel covering celebrity scandals, controversial narratives, and high-stakes conflicts. The visual language is sensational, mysterious, and captivating — conveying shocking revelations, hidden truths, and dramatic twists in the lives of public figures. Thumbnails feature striking close-ups of celebrity faces, dramatic split compositions, or symbolic imagery (cracked masks, spotlights, courtroom scenes) combined with bold, urgent text that promises scandal, betrayal, and jaw-dropping revelations. The overall vibe is intense, gossip-driven, and slightly dark — not cheerful, not academic, and never understated.

❌ Not allowed:

Bright, cheerful, or playful aesthetics (keep it dramatic and serious)

Cartoonish or childish illustrations (use real celebrity/event photography)

Cluttered frames with more than 3–4 distinct visual elements

Text that exceeds 5–6 words total on the thumbnail

Academic or dry presentation (keep it sensational and engaging)

Low-quality or blurry celebrity imagery (must look polished and impactful)

🧍‍♂️ FRAMING & POSE

Primary subject: A celebrity, public figure, or key person involved in the story — typically one of the following:

A close-up of a celebrity's face — showing a dramatic expression (shock, sadness, anger, intensity)

A split composition — two faces or two contrasting images (before/after, public vs. private, accuser vs. accused)

A symbolic image — a cracked mask, a spotlight on a dark stage, a courtroom gavel, paparazzi cameras

An object central to the scandal — a wedding ring, a contract, a car, a microphone

Framing: Close-up to medium shots. The subject occupies 50–70% of the frame. Faces are large and emotionally expressive — the viewer should feel the drama immediately.

Angle: Straight-on or slightly dramatic angles — low angles for power figures, high angles for vulnerability, or confrontational straight-on stares.

Energy: Intense, scandalous, and gripping — the imagery conveys "you won't believe what happened" and "the truth is finally coming out."

Cropping: Tight but not claustrophobic — faces fill the frame with enough room for text overlay. Cropping emphasizes emotion and intensity.

🎨 BACKGROUND & LIGHTING

Environment: Dramatic, atmospheric settings — typically:

Dark or moody backgrounds — black, deep red, dark blue, or gradient shadows

Studio-style lighting — dramatic spotlights, silhouettes, high-contrast chiaroscuro

Symbolic environments — a courtroom, a red carpet, a press conference, a private room

News-style graphics — subtle overlays suggesting "breaking news" or "exclusive" (used sparingly)

Lighting: High-contrast, dramatic lighting — strong shadows, selective highlights, often with a single dramatic light source (spotlight, window light, flash photography). Creates a sense of revelation and exposure.

Depth: Moderate — layered compositions with foreground (subject), midground (contextual elements), and background (dark atmosphere). Creates a sense of depth and intrigue.

Visual noise: Low to moderate — backgrounds are atmospheric but not cluttered, allowing the subject and text to dominate.

🧭 IDENTIFICADORES / SÍMBOLOS DO CANAL

Recurring visual symbols:

Celebrity faces — the primary visual element across most thumbnails

Split imagery — before/after, public/private, two opposing figures

Dramatic lighting effects — spotlights, shadows, silhouettes

Symbolic objects — wedding rings, contracts, gavels, microphones, cameras

News-style elements — subtle "breaking" or "exclusive" indicators (rare)

Contrasting color palettes — warm vs. cool, light vs. dark, to emphasize conflict

Placement: Symbols are integrated naturally into the scene. The celebrity face is the star; additional elements provide context and drama.

Color symbolism:

Dark/Black — mystery, scandal, the unknown

Red — danger, passion, conflict, urgency

Gold/Yellow — spotlight, revelation, fame

Blue — cold truth, exposure, the public eye

🚨 VISUAL ATTENTION ELEMENT

Usage: Minimal and strategic. The channel relies on the celebrity's face, the dramatic lighting, and the sensational text to grab attention.

If used:

A subtle glow or highlight around the celebrity's face or a key element

A simple arrow or circle pointing to a specific detail (rare)

A cracked or shattered effect on an image to suggest a breaking story

No emojis, no neon effects, no excessive arrows

Rule: The "attention" comes from the celebrity's expression and the promise of scandal — not from visual gimmicks. Keep it dramatic but tasteful.

📝 TEXT STYLE

Structure:

Maximum 2 lines of text

Positioned prominently — often centered at the bottom, lower third, or along the side

Text occupies approximately 15–20% of the thumbnail area

Typography:

Bold, clean, sans-serif font (e.g., Montserrat, Impact, or similar heavy font) — readable and impactful

Primary text: White or bright yellow/red, with a strong dark outline or drop shadow for contrast against dramatic backgrounds

Secondary text (if used): Smaller, in a contrasting color (red for urgency, gold for revelation)

Hierarchy:

Line 1: Primary hook (largest, boldest) — 3–5 words max

Line 2: Supporting context (smaller) — 2–4 words max

Background for text: Often a semi-transparent dark overlay or gradient fade at the bottom of the image to ensure text readability against varied backgrounds.

🎯 COMPOSITION RULES

Rule of thirds: Primary subject (celebrity face, split composition, or symbolic image) placed centrally or along the vertical thirds. Text occupies the bottom third.

Focal hierarchy:

Primary: The celebrity's face or the dramatic image (emotional hook)

Secondary: Text (the promise/hook)

Tertiary: Supporting visual details (lighting, symbols, context)

Negative space: Atmospheric dark space provides natural negative space. Text and subjects are placed to avoid overlapping in ways that reduce readability.

Distraction control: Maximum 3–4 distinct visual elements total (subject, text, 1–2 supporting elements like lighting or symbols).

Mobile-first: Text must be large enough to read on a smartphone screen. The celebrity's face must be clearly identifiable even at small sizes.
