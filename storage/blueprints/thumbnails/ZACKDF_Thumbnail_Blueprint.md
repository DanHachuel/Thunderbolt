🧍‍♂️ FRAMING & POSE

Primary subject: A single, dominant focal point — typically one of the following:

A dramatic cross-section or cutaway of the main subject, revealing internal structures with extreme scientific detail

A human figure or body part in a realistic, often unsettling scenario (e.g., a skull with an arrow through it, a hand being plucked, a brain being examined)

An object or scene rendered in photorealistic 3D with dramatic lighting (e.g., a black hole, a prison escape, a historical event)

A hyper-realistic close-up of the video's central element (e.g., a nose hair being plucked, a finger blocking a gun)

Framing: Center-focused composition. The subject occupies 60–80% of the frame, filling the center with extreme detail. Tight, intimate framing that leaves no doubt about what the video is about.

Angle: Dramatic, IMAX-level perspective — often close-up, slightly below or at eye level, creating an immersive and intense viewing experience.

Energy: Intense, gripping, and slightly disturbing — the imagery conveys "you need to see this" and "this will shock you."

Cropping: Tight — subjects fill most of the frame with minimal breathing room. Cropping emphasizes detail and drama.

🎨 BACKGROUND & LIGHTING

Environment: Dark, documentary-style backgrounds — typically:

Dark, moody, or black backgrounds with subtle atmospheric elements

Volumetric light and depth of field effects that create cinematic depth

Documentary realism — the background exists only to support the subject, never to distract

Lighting: Dramatic, high-contrast cinematic lighting — featuring:

Dramatic rim lighting that highlights the edges of the subject

High contrast between light and shadow for maximum visual impact

Volumetric light effects that add depth and atmosphere

Depth: Strong depth of field — the subject is in sharp focus while the background remains slightly out of focus, creating a cinematic, documentary feel.

Visual noise: Very low — backgrounds are dark and minimal, allowing the photorealistic subject to dominate the frame completely.

🎯 COMPOSITION RULES

Rule of thirds: Subject is center-focused — the main element occupies the center of the frame, filling most of the space. Text occupies the bottom third.

Focal hierarchy:

Primary: The photorealistic subject (the "wow" factor — shocking, fascinating, or disturbing)

Secondary: Text (the hook/promise)

Tertiary: Lighting and atmospheric effects (depth, drama, cinematic quality)

Negative space: Minimal — the subject fills most of the frame, with dark atmospheric space around the edges.

Distraction control: Maximum 2–3 distinct visual elements total (subject, text, 1 atmospheric element like light rays).

Mobile-first: Text must be large enough to read on a smartphone screen. The subject must be instantly recognizable and shocking even at small sizes.
