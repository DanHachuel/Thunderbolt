🧍‍♂️ FRAMING & POSE

Framing: Wide, cinematic shots that emphasize scale and drama. The primary subject occupies 50–70% of the frame, with the remaining space used for text and atmospheric elements.

Energy: Intense, dramatic, and curiosity-driven — the imagery conveys "you won't believe what these vehicles can do" and "there's a fascinating story behind this."

Cropping: Subjects are framed with breathing room for text placement, typically at the bottom or on one side.

Visual noise: Low to moderate — backgrounds are atmospheric but not cluttered, allowing the primary subject and text to stand out.

🎯 COMPOSITION RULES

Rule of thirds: Primary subject (vehicle, component, extreme scene) placed along one of the vertical or horizontal thirds. Text occupies the negative space — often at the bottom or on the side.

Focal hierarchy:

Primary: The dramatic vehicle or mechanical imagery (the "wow" factor)

Secondary: Text (the hook/promise)

Tertiary: Supporting visual details (environment, weather, scale elements)

Negative space: Atmospheric backgrounds provide natural negative space. Text and subjects are placed to avoid overlapping in ways that reduce readability.

Distraction control: Maximum 3–4 distinct visual elements total (primary subject, text, 1–2 supporting elements like weather or environment).

Mobile-first: Text must be large enough to read on a smartphone screen. The primary subject must be clearly identifiable even at small sizes.
